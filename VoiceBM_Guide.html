Start here

What VoiceBM is

VoiceBM is local voice biometrics for Home Assistant. It listens to live audio, decides who is speaking, and hands that answer to your home — entirely on your own hardware, with nothing sent to the cloud.

It runs next to your speech-to-text, not instead of it. Speech-to-text turns sound into words. VoiceBM turns sound into a name. Each result is published to Home Assistant, where your automations decide what to do with it.

The design is deliberately plain: VoiceBM is a set of small, independent pieces, each with one job, each publishing its result as a message your home can read. How you use those results — to greet someone, route a request, personalize a response, or gate access — is entirely up to you. VoiceBM provides the answer; you decide what it means.

Underneath, VoiceBM is really an orchestrator. The pieces that do the heavy lifting — the engine that turns a voice into a print, the speech-to-text it runs alongside, the model that reads tone — are components it coordinates, not parts welded into it, and any of them can be swapped for another with little effort. That separation is why VoiceBM stays light: it runs on CPU by default, asks for no GPU and no CUDA, and never depends on a particular machine-learning stack. GPU acceleration is something you can add to a component yourself if you want it — never something assumed.

The premise

It answers one question

Every part of VoiceBM exists to answer “who just spoke?” — and nothing more. It does not transcribe for you, it does not run your assistant, and it does not decide what anyone is allowed to do. It identifies the speaker and announces the result.

Think of it the way you'd think of a motion sensor. A motion sensor detects movement; what you build on top of that — a light, an alert, an alarm — is your decision, not the sensor's. VoiceBM is the same idea, pointed at voices instead of motion. It recognizes a person; everything downstream is yours to design.

A clear boundary

What it is — and what it isn't

VoiceBM is identity resolution: it recognizes voices it has been taught and flags the ones it hasn't.

VoiceBM is not a security system or an access-control product. It will tell you a voice belongs to a known person; it will not tell you whether that person should be allowed to do something. If you want access rules, you build them yourself, using VoiceBM's answer as one input among several — exactly the way you'd wire a motion sensor into an alarm rather than calling the sensor an alarm.

Worth remembering

Recognizing a voice and trusting a voice are two different things. VoiceBM does the first. The second is a decision your automations make.

How it works

Two ways it listens

VoiceBM listens on two tracks at once. They do different jobs and you should never confuse one for the other.

On demand

Active identification

Runs the moment someone speaks to your assistant. It checks the voice against everyone you've enrolled, then publishes a name, a confidence score, and a decision — before the words are handed off. This is the authoritative answer. When an automation needs to know who is talking, this is the track to trust.

Always on

Background listening

Runs continuously on each audio source. It records short clips, discards anything that isn't speech, and quietly scores what's left against your known voices. Its job is to help you build and review your roster over time — not to make live decisions. Treat it as ambient observation, not a verdict.

Don't mix them up

Background scoring is a running estimate and can be out of date by design. Use active identification when an automation needs a real answer about who is speaking right now.

Teaching it voices

Voiceprints and your roster

VoiceBM recognizes a person by their voiceprint — a compact mathematical signature of how their voice sounds, a little like a fingerprint for speech. Your collection of voiceprints is your roster: the set of people the system knows.

You add people through review. When an unfamiliar voice is heard, the clip is held in a short pending list. From the Voice Biometrics device in Home Assistant you can listen to the sample, give it a name, and add it to your roster. Newly added voices are recognized immediately — no restart, no rebuild.

You're never recognizing from a single sample forever. As someone is heard again, fresh voiceprints are added, and an optional per-person limit keeps only the most recent ones so a voice can drift naturally over time without the roster growing without bound.

The result

Who's speaking — three outcomes

Every moment of listening lands in exactly one of three states. These three cover every real situation, by design.

nothing yet

The ready state. No one has spoken; there is nothing to report. This is the clean, idle value.

user

Someone spoke, but the voice doesn't match anyone on your roster. This is the catch-all — the bucket for every unrecognized voice, whether it's a stranger, a passing sound, or simply someone you haven't enrolled yet. It doesn't matter why it didn't match; if it spoke and wasn't recognized, it lands here.

a known name

Someone spoke and the voice matched a person on your roster. They are now identified by name, and your home can respond to exactly who they are.

When you enroll the person who was showing up as user, you're not renaming the catch-all — you're creating a new known identity. The catch-all stays exactly where it is, ready for the next unrecognized voice.

The part you'll tune

Confidence, thresholds, and getting it right

Each time VoiceBM hears a voice, it scores how closely that voice matches each person it knows. A match only counts if the score clears a threshold — a confidence bar that you control.

There are two levels of bar working together:

  • A baseline bar that every match must clear to be considered a match at all.
  • On top of that, each person can have their own bar, set higher than the baseline.

That difference is the important part. A voice can be similar enough to clear the baseline in general, yet still not be close enough to be called a specific person. That's how VoiceBM keeps one person from being mistaken for another: you raise an individual's bar until only a genuinely close match is accepted as them. Someone can sound “close enough to be somebody” without being “close enough to be you.”

This is ongoing, not set-and-forget

With only a handful of voices enrolled, a lower bar works fine — there's little to confuse. As your roster grows, voices sit closer together, so you raise the bars to stay precise. You'll learn how your rooms and microphones behave and adjust over time. And a bar is never set to a perfect match — real voices shift between sentences, moods, distances, and microphones, so some honest slack is always left in.

The active bar is adjustable live from Home Assistant, so you can tighten or loosen recognition without touching anything else. Expect to nudge it. That's normal, and it's the single biggest lever on how the system feels.

Reading the words

The two transcript feeds

When recognition happens alongside speech-to-text, VoiceBM offers the transcript on two separate feeds. Pick the one that matches what you're building.

Use this by default

Allowed-only feed

Carries the spoken words only when the speaker is permitted. If a voice is on your filter list, this feed goes empty. No name attached — just the words, already screened. This is what most automations should listen to.

Diagnostics

Raw feed

Carries everything that was transcribed, recognized or not, with the speaker's name attached. It never goes empty just because a voice is filtered — it's the full record. Useful for debugging and for automations that do their own screening.

If you consume the raw feed, you own the cleanup: stripping the name tag and checking your filter list are your automation's responsibility, not VoiceBM's. The allowed-only feed does that work for you.

Keeping noise out

Filtering voices out

Not every voice in a room should reach your assistant. A television, a smart speaker, your own home's text-to-speech replying to itself — these are noise, and they pollute what your assistant hears. VoiceBM lets you put any recognized source on a filter list so its speech is kept out of your assistant's input.

This filter is about noise management, not security. Its purpose is to keep unwanted voices from cluttering the conversation, not to lock anyone out. The catch-all user can be filtered too — filtering it keeps every unrecognized voice out of the assistant while still letting the people you know through.

Where audio comes from

Audio sources

VoiceBM listens to audio sources. A source is an RTSP audio stream — and that's the only thing that matters about it. It might come from a dedicated microphone, a small single-board device, a satellite, or anything else that can provide a stream. The hardware behind it is irrelevant; VoiceBM only cares that audio arrives.

You can run as many sources as you like, and each is independent. Adding one is a single step that registers the stream and stands up everything that source needs on its own — nothing about your other sources changes. Background listening happens per source, so you can cover several rooms without anything stepping on anything else.

Beyond identity

Sounds and emotion

Two optional capabilities extend VoiceBM past “who.” Each is independent — install it or don't, and the core keeps working untouched.

Environmental sounds

A background listener that recognizes sounds rather than speakers — glass breaking, a dog, a passing vehicle, weather, footsteps. It's well suited to outdoor sources, which the voice side never touches. It publishes the sounds it hears so your home can react to events in the environment, not just to people.

Tone of voice

Alongside recognizing who is speaking, VoiceBM can estimate the emotional tone of an utterance — neutral, happy, sad, angry, and the like. It's published next to the identity, so an assistant can take the speaker's tone into account before it responds. It's a quick read of the moment, nothing stored. Like the rest of VoiceBM it runs on CPU — it asks for no GPU.

The intent

Built for a home you talk to

There are three ways people interact with a smart home: a command line, a dashboard you tap, and your voice. VoiceBM is built for the last one.

Recognition arrives while your assistant is already listening, so the house can respond to who is speaking without anyone reaching for a screen. The dashboard exists for setup and troubleshooting — it's a workshop, not where the system is meant to live day to day. The goal is a home that simply knows you when you speak and acts on it, quietly, on its own.

The shape of it

Voice in, recognition out, the house responds. If you find yourself tapping a card to make something work, that's a sign there's an automation still waiting to be written — not the way it's meant to run.

The interface

What it publishes

Everything VoiceBM produces is published as a message on a named channel that Home Assistant reads. You consume the channels you care about. The names below use {person} and {source} as stand-ins for your own values.

Identity — live
voicebm/active/identityThe current speaker: name, confidence, and the match decision.
voicebm/current_speakerThe display name of the person currently identified.
{person}/voiceOn while that specific person is speaking; off otherwise.
voicebm/pending_activeRecent unrecognized clips, waiting for you to review and enroll.
Transcript
voicebm/transcript/preferredWords from allowed speakers only; empty when a voice is filtered.
voicebm/transcript/debugEvery transcript, name attached — the full record for diagnostics.
Filtering
voicebm/blocklist/{person}Whether one person is filtered out of the assistant's input.
voicebm/blocklist_stateThe whole filter list at once, plus a count of who's filtered.
Tuning
voicebm/gallery_maxHow many voiceprints to keep per person before the oldest is dropped.
voicebm/active/lead_trimMilliseconds trimmed off the start of a clip, to skip a wake chime.
Background & add-ons
voicebm/{source}/resultBackground recognition for one source — an estimate, not a verdict.
voicebm/ambient/stateThe dominant environmental sound just detected.
voicebm/emote/stateThe estimated emotional tone of the latest utterance.

Read the messages on these channels directly. They're fast, they carry full detail, and they're the cleanest way to wire VoiceBM into your automations.

If something's off

Getting your bearings

Everything resolves to “user”

That means no one is being recognized. Confirm you've enrolled at least one voice, and check the active confidence bar in Home Assistant — if it's set too strict for your room and microphone, even known voices won't clear it. Loosen it and try again.

The right people aren't being told apart

Two voices are landing on each other. Raise the individual bar on the person being mistaken for someone else, and add a few more samples of each so their voiceprints are well represented. This is the tuning loop — expect to repeat it as your roster grows.

A voice keeps interrupting the assistant

A television, speaker, or your own home's replies are reaching the assistant. Add that source to the filter list so its speech is kept out, while the people you know still come through.

Recognition feels stale

You may be reading the background track where you want the live one. For a real answer about who is speaking right now, consume the live identity channel, which runs on demand when someone speaks.
